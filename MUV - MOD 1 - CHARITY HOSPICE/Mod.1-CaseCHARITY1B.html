SCENARIO 1B: Community Hospice Charity

Organisation type Local hospice providing end-of-life care, bereavement support and day services

What this module teaches: Start within. Find where energy already exists. Check authenticity before you scale.

About This Scenario

This community hospice scenario is a practical movement marketing strategy example designed for small fundraising teams who need to grow supporter engagement without large advertising budgets.

Community-led marketing principles apply powerfully here; the volunteers, families and staff who already believe in the work are the most credible and cost-effective channel available.

Authentic brand marketing for hospice charities starts with one honest truth: what is already happening is more powerful than anything you could manufacture. This 30-day plan shows exactly how to find, focus and amplify it.

The Belief

Hospices have some of the deepest community energy of any charity (retail volunteers, memorial donors, bereavement groups, legacy givers, staff who stay for decades) but it is often invisible or taken for granted. The audit surfaces where this energy already lives so the hospice can focus on what is genuinely true.

30 Day Movement Marketing Plan

Week 1: Start Within
Day 1 to 2: Run the Movement Potential Audit with 10 insiders. A mix of ward volunteers, shop volunteers, bereaved family supporters and one clinician. Map people, stories, partners, and habits. Output: completed 1 page audit.
Day 3 to 4: Run the Authenticity and Issue Fit Check. Pay attention to dignity language (is it reflected in actual care?), we are here for everyone claims (is service truly accessible?) and financial sustainability messaging (is the funding gap real and stated honestly?). Output: completed one-page fit check.
Day 5 to 7: Based on the audit, choose your six strands. Example: patient dignity stories (consent led), volunteer pride, bereavement peer support, clinical excellence, community events, legacy and memorial.
Week 2: Tell the Stories That Are Already True
Day 8 to 10: From the audit's strongest niches, collect 8 proof stories. Four from volunteers ('why I have stayed 12 years') and four from families (consent-led, anonymised where needed: 'what the hospice meant to our family'). Audio preferred. The human voice carries hospice stories better than text.
Day 11 to 14: Create a Community Guide called "What Your Hospice Does". In print, PDF and audio. Not a leaflet about services. A simple, warm explanation of what happens here, who is cared for, how it is funded and how anyone can be part of it. Designed so GP surgeries, pharmacies and community centres will display it.
Week 3: Build the Bridge
Day 15 to 17: Host an Open Hospice Morning. Invite 30 community members (a mix of existing supporters and people who have never visited). Tours in small groups led by volunteers, refreshments, one family story shared live (with consent). Design it so people leave saying 'I had no idea', not 'that was sad'
Day 18 to 21: Capture 10 reflections. Publish as a story thread. Ask every attendee to choose one action: volunteer, donate monthly, share or attend the next event.
Week 4: Amplify and Sustain
Day 22 to 25: Launch monthly Hospice Heartbeat email. One patient or family story, one volunteer spotlight, one funding fact (what ten pounds covers), one event invitation.
Day 26 to 28: Distribute Community Guide through 5 GP surgeries and 3 pharmacies. Partner with one local business for a cross promotion.
Day 29 to 30: Baseline and review signals. Book the next Open Morning date.

Why This Saves You Money and Still Grows

Less waste, more reuse
Community Guide and Open Morning become quarterly fixtures. No reinvention.
Partners distribute for you
GP surgeries and pharmacies put your guide in waiting rooms. Free and trusted.
Proof beats polish
Real volunteer stories and family reflections outperform charity advertising.
Emotional truth converts
When someone walks through the hospice, trust is built in 30 minutes.

How This Gets You More Donors

Awareness becomes involvement
Open Morning creates an emotional connection. The monthly email sustains it.
Trust builds faster
Seeing the care, meeting the volunteers and understanding the funding model removes all barriers.
Repeat loop
Monthly email plus quarterly Open Morning plus GP guide distribution equals compounding community awareness.

Tracking: Non-Negotiable

Baseline (Day 0): donor count, monthly income, volunteer enquiries and leading indicators (Open Morning RSVPs, guide distribution take up, email signups). Review weekly for 15 minutes.

How a 30 Day Plan Becomes a Movement

A movement forms when 'this is our hospice' becomes a community identity people carry, not just something they donate to at Christmas. That builds donor resilience because supporters understand the funding model, visit the hospice and advocate year round.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How does movement marketing work for a hospice charity?
A: It shifts the hospice from 'we need funding' to 'this is your hospice and here is how it works', building participation through Open Mornings, proof stories and a Community Guide that partners distribute on your behalf.
Q: What is community-led marketing for charities?
A: It means your volunteers, supporters and families carry the message rather than the organisation broadcasting alone. When people experience something genuine, like an Open Morning, they naturally tell others.
Q: Is authentic hospice brand marketing different from charities?
A: Yes - the fit check is more critical here. Every claim must be tested against dignity, accessibility and financial honesty before being communicated publicly.
